One image that sums up the world's news of Tuesday 17 November 2026: a drawn newspaper front page carrying 39 verified stories — twelve illustrated main stories led by a dominant hero panel on Ukraine's strike collapsing part of the Kerch Bridge, plus a 27-story "Also on Tuesday" one-liner wire. The mains: tankers hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz (an IRGC claim), Trump landing in Manila for the ASEAN summit, Nvidia's first earnings since Jensen Huang stepped down, the global Microsoft 365 outage, Japan scrambling jets over Russian aircraft, Western nations urging Israel to open Gaza crossings, 128 states debating lethal autonomous weapons in Geneva, the EU charging Temu over illegal goods, the COP31 draft calling time on fossil fuels, Ebola in DR Congo still not under control, and India winning the U19 Asia Cup in a last-ball thriller. A "Day in Numbers" strip (11,200 Ebola cases; 5,700+ US measles infections; 146,000 marching in France; the 66–52 Knesset vote; Voyager 1 at one light-day), a stories-by-region tally, and a numbered dot-matrix world map — with one colour per region shared by the kicker chips, map pins and list numbers — make the day's breadth auditable at a glance. Every story was verified against wire or official sources reporting it as 17 November 2026 news; disputed claims are attributed.

The entry program (`main.py`, Python standard library only) copies the committed `entry.png` byte-for-byte to `/output/entry.png` and verifies its size and signature, so every run writes identical bytes. The poster itself was drawn programmatically with `draw.py` (Pillow) — real rendered text, no generated-image garbling.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
